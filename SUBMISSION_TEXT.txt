Project title:
Recurrent Memory Lab: Learning from Demonstrations Without Growing the State

Selected topic:
In-Context Learning with Recurrent Memory

One-sentence claim:
Adding demonstrations can be absorbed into a fixed-size recurrent state, but overlapping evidence can interfere and cause retrieval errors.

Audience:
Advanced undergraduate / early graduate data-science and machine-learning learners familiar with vectors, dot products, and basic Transformer attention.

Artifact pitch:
A browser-native interactive laboratory where learners write demonstrations into a fixed 5x5 recurrent associative state, query that state, see ground truth beside the estimate, and deliberately induce interference by increasing key similarity and adding a conflicting demonstration.

BDH-CQ connection:
BDH-CQ is a 2026 Pathway research model combining in-context learning with recurrent latent reasoning. Its report describes inference-time inputs continuously updating recurrent memory, followed by iterative latent computation for the query without verbalizing intermediate reasoning. The demo teaches the state-update and retrieval portion at toy scale, and labels the abstraction boundary explicitly.

Evidence boundary:
The live computation is synthetic and independent. It is not an official BDH/BDH-CQ checkpoint and makes no benchmark claim. Research claims are sourced to primary papers in SOURCES.md.
